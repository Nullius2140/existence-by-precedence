Simple assessment
It contains a useful, concrete proposal for recording AI commitments, surrounded by a much less convincing argument that Bitcoin’s incentives would protect humans in a machine economy.

The writing is compelling. But several passages present conditional economic arguments as though they had the certainty of arithmetic or physics.

What is strong
The essay correctly distinguishes trusting intentions from designing institutions. Public, independently checkable records of safety commitments could improve accountability. Its proposed timestamping experiment is practical and separable from its larger monetary thesis.

It also deserves credit for naming substantial weaknesses: uncertain adoption, unequal ownership, the singleton scenario, attack-and-short incentives, and the lack of experimental results. It explicitly says this does not replace alignment.

Where it overreaches
Protecting a ledger does not necessarily protect its human users. Machines could maintain reliable payments among themselves while coercing humans into signing transfers or denying them essential goods. Bitcoin would record valid transactions without detecting the coercion. That is a multipolar counterexample, not the singleton exception the essay already acknowledges. “Neutrality is indivisible” needs an argument that excludes such arrangements; the essay does not supply one.
Greater intelligence does not automatically make honest participation optimal. The guardian argument depends on what agents want, what they own, and what they gain outside Bitcoin. An agent might value strategic control or damage to competitors more than its mining revenue. The essay acknowledges one version through attack-and-short, but that concession weakens its sweeping claim that intelligence necessarily hardens the constitution. Better calculation cannot fix a payoff structure that rewards harm.
The pension integral’s conclusion is too broad. Purchasing power growing faster than consumption does not, by itself, guarantee a finite lifetime expenditure. With constant consumption and prices falling as

The physics language overstates Bitcoin’s guarantees. Reorganizing a blockchain does not reverse thermodynamics—it substitutes a competing transaction history. Proof-of-work provides conditional, probabilistic security, not physically irreversible ownership. Likewise, calling a cryptographic break “systemically harmless” because stolen coins move with valid signatures overlooks the system’s central purpose: protecting ownership. A valid signature also does not inherently reveal whether a key was stolen, computed, or legitimately used.
An anchored transaction does not establish an uncopiable machine identity. Copies can possess the same key and claim the same preceding history. Spending energy creates an event that cannot be unspent, but it does not uniquely identify which software instance caused it. Similarly, a final transfer records behavior, not an unambiguous revelation of the system’s underlying values.
Bottom line
Worth reading as a provocative institutional essay; worth testing as an accountability mechanism; not established as an economic safeguard for humanity.

Its strongest contribution is “make commitments publicly checkable.” Its weakest move is repeatedly treating the jump from a durable record to durable human protection as something the record itself supplies. That missing connection is substantive—not merely a demand that the proposal solve every possible problem.
