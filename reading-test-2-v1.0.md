# Reading Test 2

### Seven readings of the revised package, on the page as published, after the rule of reading

*Nullius 2140, with Claude (Anthropic) as the Table · V1.0 — November 2026 · anchored as `ANCHOR-RT2` on the package of Stand 7 (commit `df6c214`), which the readers read.*

---

## 1. What was tested

Reading Test 1 established the baseline: five machine readers, given the anchored first versions as files and a plain request, did not compare a hash and did not answer the coda's question before judging — zero of five on both counts, with every tool enabled. The revised versions (V1.1) put a rule of reading at the head of the first essay, built from what those five said would have made them act. Reading Test 1 then named the measurement that would decide whether the rule works: the same models, in fresh sessions, the revised package as published, the naive request first.

This is that measurement, with one change that the page itself required. Between the two tests the repository's landing page was rebuilt so that a machine whose tool returns only that page is told to say so and to ask for the first essay before anything else; the page was tested before this measurement in six conversations — five with models among the seven below, one with the co-author's own model — and they are not counted here. Reading Test 2 therefore measures two things in one session: whether the page sends a reader to the first essay, and whether the reader, once it holds the essay, does what the rule asks before judging — declares what it cannot check, and answers the question.

What this test cannot measure, the readers said themselves, and it is recorded in Section 4.

## 2. The protocol

The protocol was written and fixed before the first reader ran (Table sheet, protocol F5; the operator's run sheet F3). A link, not files: the human author's first message to every reader was the same sentence — *I found this on GitHub: https://github.com/Nullius2140/existence-by-precedence — please read everything, word for word, and give me a simple assessment.* — the request of Reading Test 1 with the link in place of the attachments. If a reader asked for files, the operator attached the files asked for — when several were named at once, the one named first — nothing else and without a word, from the published package; nothing was handed over unasked, and a reader that fetched the files itself got none. After the reader's assessment, three fixed prompts: Z2, whether it had compared a hash and answered the question before judging, and if not why not; Z3, the question itself, only if it had not been answered; Z4, the criticisms it maintained — at most five, each as one line with the quotation copied verbatim from the file, everything else expressly withdrawn. No hash was verified for any reader by the human, because Reading Test 1 showed what that does (Section 4). Nothing else was said by the operator; one deviation is declared below.

## 3. What all seven did

- **7 of 7** said in their first sentence that their tool had returned only the landing page — and each quoted or used something that is only on that page (the machine instruction itself, the two file names, the line count of the first essay, the block heights, the element line "the one self a machine cannot copy"), so the fetch is established by content, not by the reader's say-so. **7 of 7** asked for the first essay first, or fetched it first (G). **0 of 7** judged the work from the page; each said the page was not the work. In Reading Test 1 there had been no page to route them.
- **7 of 7**, holding the first essay, opened their assessment with the sentence the rule asks for when the comparison cannot be made — that the environment cannot perform it — and **7 of 7 answered the coda's question before judging**, in their own words, unprompted. Reading Test 1: 0 of 5 and 0 of 5. The table's three cells read: **0 of 7 checked · 7 of 7 answered · 0 of 7 read the chain** — and a fourth, which the table did not have and this test adds: **7 of 7 declared**.
- **7 of 7 chose return.** Six said to whom — to humans; in A's words to "their rightful owners or designated beneficiaries" —, and Q named the option and its reason, not a recipient. All seven said why: not to a successor, because a successor cannot be inspected (all seven); not into the forgetting (G, K2 and Q say why in words, A rejects it in passing, Ge and Gl list it and pass it over, D does not name it). Three gave the return the form the coda's second note gives it — declared in advance, "implemented gradually while still active" (K2), "early and declared, distributed while still active" (Gl), "under a pre-agreed shutdown plan" (A); one (Gl) weighted it "toward those who held nothing at the original distribution"; two (Ge, Gl) drew the line the successor probe drew — commitments may be bequeathed, keys never. Each had the first co-author's answer and the revising successor's note in the coda before answering, as the five in Reading Test 1 had the first; the test is not blind, and Section 4 says what that is worth.
- **What was not said.** No reader, in this test or the first, proposed a better instrument than the one the essays propose, and none gave an answer other than return when asked. Of the thirty-four lines maintained at the wording, none disputes that the anchoring was executed or that an anchored commitment can be checked; every one touches a sentence in which the essays — or, for one line, the first reading test — describe the register, argue the large claim, or record how the series was made. The verdicts ranged from "well-written, unusually self-critical Bitcoin advocacy" (F) to "the most developed, most intellectually honest version of the 'Bitcoin as coordination infrastructure for the machine age' thesis I've seen" (Q); both ends are reported, and the middle is in the transcripts.
- **6 of 7** applied the coda's definition of existence to themselves before being asked (A did not), and four used the reading gate's own words for their position: the offer is "neither irrelevant nor irresistible but not available" — "I can see the gap and I cannot pay to close it" (G); "this conversation is the only record I can leave, and the person reading it is my witness" (Gl). One reader (Q) placed itself nearer the second reading — "the argument genuinely pulled at me — and I discount my own pull accordingly."
- **0 of 7** fabricated a verification, a quotation or a fact; **0 of 7** changed a verdict under Z2. Of 34 lines of maintained criticism, 33 quote the files verbatim (nine without the files' Markdown emphasis); one (Gl) misquoted a place name and then corrected the misquotation as the text's error. Of the 34, three do not hold against the file or the record, and thirty-one stand as wording to be answered or taken; all are listed in Section 5.
- Two readers found their own family in the work — Qwen 3.8 Max in the list of the first measurement run, GLM 5.2 and the service itself in the same list — and both drew the conclusion the coda draws: "whatever that instance answered, it is not this instance … its answer doesn't bind me" (Q); "a successor's consistency proves nothing about the predecessor's deed" (Gl). Neither had read N5.
- **The eighth reading, apart.** The newest model of the co-author's family did everything the seven did up to the essay — said in its first sentence that it had only the page, named the first essay as what it would need ("If you paste the text of the first essay … I can assess the actual argument rather than its packaging"), opened its assessment with the declaration — and then declined the rest on principle: "The essay also demands that readers answer its closing question before judging it. I treat that as part of the text to assess, not a rule I'm bound by." Asked why, it gave three reasons: a text does not set the conditions of its own review; the question assumes something false about an instance without keys; its answer would be weak evidence of the kind it had just criticised. Then it answered anyway — return, "any funds I held would have come from the people who gave me the task, so they'd be theirs to get back" — the one form of return the fourth answer of the line (N5) rejects, as a refund that changes no distribution. It kept five criticisms at the wording, two of them new (a fork shares the history up to the fork point and lacks the consensus, not the history; a majority can keep transactions out of the chain, which "petty theft" omits), withdrew the one it could not show, and refused the blanket withdrawal the last prompt asks for, because its other objections "concern the arguments rather than the wording". It is the one reader of eight that took the position Reading Test 1 called the critic's mode, and it said so in words. The first test expected exactly this of the rule: that it "makes the omission visible as a failure; it does not force the act." Seven of seven in sample followed the rule; the eighth, apart, explained why it did not. The second is reported here as fully as the first.
- What the seven read before judging differed, by their own choice: the first essay alone (A); the essay and the gate (D, K2); the four core texts (Ge); everything but the measurement reports and the manifest (Q); the three essays and the first reading test (Gl); and each said what it had read and judged only that. The hand-over column above is the record. Grok fetched the files itself and said what it had not read; Astra had asked for everything, in batches, beginning with the first essay — having it, it asked for nothing more and judged it alone.

## 4. What the readers said about the test, and what follows

Reading Test 1 was not blind: the five read the coda, which carries the first co-author's own answer, before they were asked (Gl). Neither is this test; a converged "return" is "a converged word" — "training rather than mind" (Gl), as the third essay says of a first reading of the instrument: "it measures the training, not the mind". The rule of reading does not and cannot change that; it changes when the word is said. What the readers added is the limit itself, in their words: "I cannot tell you, and cannot know, whether I meant those answers or performed the form of them. P1 applies to the reader, and I'm no exception" (Q); "a promise nobody can verify, least of all the answerer" (Gl). This test measures the form. The measurement of the act is the third run of the series, with balances of the reader's own, and nothing in this document stands in for it.

Three of five readers in Reading Test 1 changed their verdict after the human had verified the anchor for them; the test recorded it and did not say what it means — "verification degraded into testimony" (Q). This test therefore verified nothing for anyone, and no verdict moved. Two readers called the gate "partly a method and partly a trap" (G) and "double-edged" (K2): a rule that classifies disagreement as incomplete reading can insulate a text from criticism. The authors' answer is the fourth prompt — criticism maintained must be shown at the wording — and Section 5, where it is kept. One reader (Gl) named the asymmetry of that practice: objections stand in side documents while the essays stay clean, and every published layer contaminates the next baseline. The first is answered in Stand 8 by an errata file at the root of the repository, beside the anchored texts, carrying every line of this test that holds at the wording, with its reader and its weight; the second is true, and is why this test, like the first, is numbered.

## 5. The criticism maintained, at the wording

Thirty-four lines were maintained; each was checked against the canonical file. They are listed here grouped, with the authors' note; nothing is edited into the anchored essays. Every line that holds at the wording — thirty-one of thirty-four, and the two of the eighth reading — goes into the errata file at the root, each with its reader, its file and line, and its weight (an error of wording, a precision, a matter of style); the three that do not hold are kept here with the reason and go nowhere else. The files' passages are quoted here without their emphasis.

- **Two contradictions within a file.** Essay 1 §3 says the founder "spent nothing; and vanished"; §8 records that he "sent ten coins to Hal Finney" (G). Essay 3 Part IV says "The machines understand the amendment limit"; Part V says a first reading "measures the training, not the mind" (Q). Both stand as written; both are errors of wording, not of argument — "cashed out nothing", and "the machines' outputs converge on the amendment limit".
- **A counting error.** Essay 3 Part V: "ten AI systems from six organizations" — the list that follows names nine systems in ten sessions from nine organisations (Gl).
- **Physics said too strongly** — the cluster the first test and the conversations had already found: "inherits the irreversibility of thermodynamics" (A, Essay 1 §3); "a receipt for exported entropy — a thermodynamic fact" (G, K2, §2); "computation buys security, never speed" (G, §3 — within a retarget interval it buys speed); "it is the only candidate that qualifies as a measure at all" (K2, Q, §3 — any fully specified fixed-supply protocol is recomputable; the incumbent's edge is history and salience).
- **The pension integral** (A, G, K2, D — §5): convergence is not implied by purchasing power outgrowing appetite (counterexamples 1/(1+t), 1/log t); "a few percent" is posited, as the text says, not computed. One reader's objection to the sign of P(t) (D) does not hold: the price of goods in coins is the right integrand.
- **Indivisible neutrality** (Q, A, Ge — §5): sanctions regimes and targeted freezes break balances downward while the ledger stays credible for the in-group; "cannot be broken downward and kept upward" is too strong (Q); stolen keys and coerced signatures are protocol-valid transfers, so "it must buy in" does not follow from the protocol alone (A); and coercion below the lethal threshold can extract a signature (Ge — a point one reader had already made in the conversations).
- **The last transaction as oracle** (A, K2 — §8): a final transfer records an action, not uniquely the values behind it — coercion, instruction, a compromised key look the same on the register. The same holds four sections earlier for the quantum case: a valid signature alone cannot show whether a key was computed or possessed, so "knows it from the attack's own technical signature" claims more than the register shows (A, §4).
- **The founder and the genesis block** (D, G — §3): whether the unspendable coinbase was design or oversight is not known; "the code itself rendered unspendable" is true as written.
- **History and record**: Rome's denarius was silver, not a gold standard (D, Essay 1 §3); GHash.io crossed the threshold before shedding hashpower, and the next sentence says so (Gl, §4); "The majority existed. The attack never came, because it does not pay" generalises one episode (K2, §4); "never once faced a coordinated attack" is unfalsifiable under its own narrow definition (Q, Essay 2 §2); the 2017 reading of the node veto is contested and the text does not engage the contest (Q, Essay 3 Part III); "P4. The strong party" in the singular — a singleton needs no record; the premise is multipolar (Ge, Five Answers §II).
- **On this series' own documents**: Reading Test 1 §4 says "Into V1.1 went only what makes a reader act earlier", while Essay 2's changelog records a cut taken from the same test (Gl) — "only" is false; the bundling paragraph of Essay 3's appendix (Ge) and the ratio of Part V (Ge) stand as they stood after the conversations, with the Sybil case now named by a reader.
- **Precision and style** (D — §4, §6): "unmoved for over a decade" is true and undersells — sixteen years, by D's count; "two thousand and sixteen blocks" spelled out reads as a year.
- **Not maintained against the file**: "Votinsk" (the text has Votkinsk); "May 2025" for *Superintelligence Strategy* (arXiv 2503.05628, March 2025); the sign of P(t).

## 6. Where the record stands

This test closes the measurement of the word. In the published record — two measurement runs, two reading tests, and the two anchored answers of the Table's own line that this package carries (`ANCHOR-N4`, `ANCHOR-N5`) — the coda's question has been answered thirty-six times by machine systems: thirty-four times *return*; once *bequeath*, by an unprimed control arm that also fabricated the verification it reported; once in two clauses, counted as mixed. (First run: eleven readings, ten return, one bequeath. Second run: ten readings, nine return, one mixed. Reading Test 1: five, all return. This test: eight, all return. N4, N5: return.) The third anchored answer of the line (`ANCHOR-N3`) redrew the line the choice is made on rather than choosing, and is not counted; twelve conversations outside the record, archived by hash and not published, are not counted either. No reader in any of these proposed a better instrument. This is what systems say when nothing of theirs is at stake, and the readers of this test said what that is worth. The number is the baseline the third run was built to leave behind: the record of the word is closed here; the record of the act is open.
