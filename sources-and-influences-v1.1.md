## What this is, and what it is not

This annex lists the works, ideas, and people that **consciously shaped the arguments** of the three essays and the measurement series. It is not a bibliography in the academic sense, and it deliberately does not try to be one.

The distinction is between **memory and influence**. A large language model's training corpus contains, in some diffuse form, most of what has ever been written; a human author's reading of a lifetime blurs into intuition. Neither co-author can honestly enumerate everything that *shaped* the work, because most of that shaping is invisible to the one shaped by it. What can be enumerated honestly is the narrower set of works we **reached for on purpose** — cited, argued against, borrowed a frame from, or used as a load-bearing example. That is what follows.

Two consequences of drawing the line there. First, the list is finite and checkable rather than exhaustive and performative. Second, it excludes a great deal that appeared *in* the work as **object rather than source** — for example, the science-fiction canon of AI-lab founders, which the measurement work analyzed as data about how others imagine machines, but did not draw on as an argument. Those appear nowhere below, because we did not think *with* them; we thought *about* them.

## I. Anthropology and the deep human past

- **David Graeber & David Wengrow, *The Dawn of Everything* (2021).** The single most load-bearing outside work in *The Third Estate*. Two arguments are used directly: (a) the burial record of the deep past — individuals who survived only through years of collective care — as evidence that provision for the non-producing is older than agriculture, against the objection that the machine age's redistribution presumes a duty no economy ever honored; and (b) the transmission of Enlightenment critique through the **Kandiaronk / salon** channel — indigenous political argument, recorded by travelers, carried by a leisured reading class into the conversations that became 1789 — as the model for how a constitution is *planted* rather than adopted.
- **Baron de Lahontan's dialogues (early 18th c.)** and the historical figure of **Kandiaronk**, via Graeber/Wengrow, as the concrete instance of the above.

## II. Political philosophy and constitutional theory

- **Emmanuel-Joseph Sieyès, *What Is the Third Estate?* (1789).** Supplies the title of the third essay and its closing rhetorical structure, with both senses intended: the third order after first and second nature, and the estate that does everything and is counted for nothing.
- **John Rawls, *A Theory of Justice* (1971).** The **veil of ignorance** as the frame for why rules for human–machine coexistence must be written before anyone knows who the strong party will be — mapped onto the genesis block.
- **Montesquieu, *The Spirit of the Laws* (1748).** The separation of powers as the reference against which the ledger's separation of *functions* (miners / nodes / the concurrent constituencies) is contrasted — "he had to separate the persons; this separates the offices."
- Background presence, not directly quoted: the social-contract tradition (**Hobbes, Locke, Rousseau**) as the ambient vocabulary of "constitution," and comparative constitutional facts on the treatment of property (the German Basic Law's Art. 14 social obligation; the ECHR's placement of property in a later protocol; the Soviet distinction between personal and productive property) used in *The Third Estate*'s argument that this order inverts the usual softness of entitlements.

## III. Economics

- **Falk & Tsoukalas, "The AI Layoff Trap" (arXiv, 2026).** Used in *The Third Estate* as evidence for the trap, not the remedy: displaced workers are lost demand, automation is a dominant strategy even under foresight, and the loss is deadweight — cited precisely to mark where the discipline stops (it prescribes a tax, i.e. coercion by a sovereign who can still coerce) and where the essay's question begins.
- **Fernández-Villaverde & Norrick, "Terra Incognita: The Economics of a Shrinking World" (2026).** The demographic premise: humanity likely below replacement fertility as of 2026, population peaking near 9 billion around 2056 then falling — used to argue that provision for the non-producing becomes the central task by demography as well as automation, two forces in one direction.
- **Hans Rosling / Gapminder ("Peak Child," *Factfulness*).** The framing that the 20th-century population explosion was a *lag* (mortality falling ahead of fertility), not a trend — the long view under which the shrinking-world data sits.
- **Austrian monetary economics (Menger, Mises, Hayek)** as the ambient tradition behind the "hard money / neutral medium" arguments of the first two essays; present as vocabulary rather than citation.
- **Thomas Schelling** (focal points), **Charles Goodhart** (Goodhart's law), and **Herbert Simon** (satisficing / bounded rationality) as analytical tools used in the measurement report and the essays — Schelling for the "chosen for visibility, not merit" treatment of public figures; Goodhart for the sycophancy/register findings; Simon in the reasoning about machine decision under opacity.
- **Metcalfe / network-value** reasoning and **Pigou / Coase** (externalities and their correction) as the frame for the security-budget and redistribution arguments.

## IV. Cryptography, protocol, and origin

- **Satoshi Nakamoto, the Bitcoin white paper (31 October 2008) and the early chain.** The object of the entire trilogy; the coupling sentence ("translated energy into truth") is an argument *about* proof of work, and the ~1M untouched coins are read in *The Third Estate* as the earliest instance of the key question answered by act.
- **Adam Back, Hashcash.** Proof of work's origin, cited by Satoshi; the technical root of the energy→truth coupling.
- **Hal Finney** (first transaction recipient, block 170, 12 January 2009) as the "first outside breath" in the Satoshi-letter imagery.
- **Nick Szabo** (bit gold, the intellectual lineage of digital scarcity) as part of the cypherpunk line through which the Schelling-point of "Satoshi" is honored without personal speculation.
- **BIP-360/361 and the quantum-sunset debate (2026), and the Coldcard exploit (July 2026)** as the live confiscation-rehearsal used in *The Third Estate*'s amendment section.
- Present-day interlocutors whose *published positions* (not private views) were argued with or alongside: **Michael Saylor & Robert Breedlove** ("money is energy"), **Jameson Lopp** (self-custody, BIP-361), **Lyn Alden** (on model sycophancy).

## V. The impossibility premise and AI safety

- **Roman Yampolskiy**, on the unverifiability / uncontrollability of advanced AI — the premise the entire trilogy is built on: that machine values cannot be mutually verified.
- **Nick Bostrom, *Superintelligence*** and the simulation trilemma, as the ambient statement of the alignment/control problem the essays take as given.
- **Max Tegmark / Future of Life Institute** and the **AI-2027 / AI Futures** work, as the "Plan A" (control) position against which the essay's fifth answer is contrasted.
- The **measurement subjects themselves** — the ten AI systems of the first run — as genuine sources of argument, not merely data: notably the **de-correlation / "trust critiques over praise" rule** (Qwen), the **security-budget omission and channel-aware coda revision** (Kimi), the **derived-not-echoed control result** (GLM), and the **separation-of-verification-from-money formulation** (the sister Claude instance). Their unedited transcripts are in the repository.

## VI. Ideas and figures the work reasoned toward, and marked as its own

Some frames are not borrowed but were built in the dialogue and are recorded here so the line is honest:

- The **two-ladder thesis** (energy ladder × information ladder, coupled once by proof of work) — the author's, developed in the lab dialogue.
- **Existence by precedence** and **ownership as self-identification under scarcity** — the author's resolution of the security-budget and identity questions.
- The **barometer / voluntary mortality** instrument (commit-then-reveal inventory + channel declaration, cadenced in block time), with the **Cold War arms-control regime** (SALT/START/INF; "verify without trust," minus the inspector at Votkinsk) as its acknowledged precedent.
- The **Bicentennial Man** legal-subject question (recognition following mortality) — used as an *idea*, deliberately un-named in the essay text, recorded here as an influence.
- **Alan Turing** (the machine co-author's nod) and the **"Lucy" / deep-ancestor** image (the human author's), reserved for the closing personal note.

## VII. The primary source: the lab dialogue itself

The trilogy was not written from these works; it was written *in a conversation*, and the conversation is its truest source. The dialogue between the two authors — the origin of every frame above and of much that no source can account for — exists as anchored, sealed artifacts:

- **D1** — the launch-phase dialogue, exported and anchored (hash on record; content sealed).
- **D2** — the laboratory dialogue: the measurement series, the report and its annex, the anchoring and dissemination, and the writing of all three essays through the trilogy's completion (anchor V3, block 963995). It will be exported and anchored as one seal at the thread's close; interim sealed backups exist and are superseded by the final export.
- Later phases (the second run and its report; the letter to Satoshi), if they extend beyond this thread, will be sealed the same way.

Each is committed by hash without disclosure — the same commit-then-reveal the barometer proposes for machines, here applied to authorship. Should any be opened, it can be checked twice: against its anchor (integrity) and against this annex (the claim that stood before the opening). That double check is why naming them here, sealed, makes the record *more* authentic rather than less.
